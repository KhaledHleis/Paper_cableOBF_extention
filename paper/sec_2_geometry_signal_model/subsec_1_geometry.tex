\subsection{Survey Geometry}\label{subsec:geometry}

The vehicle moves at speed $v=\SI{1.0}{\metre\per\second}$ along straight legs that cross a straight cable at an incidence $\theta$ (\cref{fig:geometry}a). Two tri-axial fluxgates are mounted at vehicle's depth on the vehicle's lateral axis, UNO on the port side and DUO on the starboard side, separated by the baseline $b=\SI{1}{\metre}$. For each sensor we use a local CPA frame: the origin is the point of its track directly above the cable, $\hat{\vect{x}}$ is the horizontal normal to the cable, $\hat{\vect{y}}$ points along the cable and $\hat{\vect{z}}$ points down (\cref{fig:geometry}b). The cable then lies at $x=0$, $z=d$, where $d$ is the standoff.

A sensor that crosses the cable at time $t_c$ has horizontal distance $x=v\,(t-t_c)\sin\theta$ from the cable at time $t$. Every field quantity below depends on $x$ only through the ratio $x/d$, so we use the normalised coordinate
\begin{equation}
\tau=\frac{x}{d}=\frac{v\,(t-t_c)\sin\theta}{d}=\frac{t-t_c}{T_s},\qquad T_s=\frac{d}{v\sin\theta}.
\label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
$\tau$ counts CPA distances, and $T_s$ is the time the sensor takes to move one CPA distance across the cable. $T_s$ is the time scale of every matched template, and we call it the template scale. A leg that starts and ends at the lateral distance $A$ from the cable covers $|x|\le A$, i.e.\ $|\tau|\le K$ with
\begin{equation}
K=\frac{L}{d},\qquad L=A\ \text{for a snake leg}.
\label{eq:K}
\end{equation}
$L$ is the half-length of the pass measured across the cable. With the snake amplitude $A=\SI{6}{\metre}$ used in closed loop and $d=\SIrange{2}{4}{\metre}$, $K=3$--$1.5$: a snake leg sees only a few CPA distances on each side of the crossing.
