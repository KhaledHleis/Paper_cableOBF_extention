\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
submarine telecommunication cable carry most intercontinental data traffic, and their inspection, burial assessment, repair and protection all start with knowing where they are. Often the cable is buried, in consequence, accoustic and optical methods lose it, and electromagnetic sensing becomes the main modality that penetrates the sediments.
Submarine telecommunications cables hold DC current to power repeaters along the way, this current is about 1A. with the sea as the return path. they therefore produce a static line-current.
At a distance of 2-4m from the cable this field is 100-50 nT, three order of magnitude below the geomagnetic field of about 48000nT.

In previous work we derived two orthonormal basis functions called cable OBF, matched to an inifinite current. the resulting generalised likelihood ratio test (GLRT) detects the cable with fewer noise dimensions, and places the closest point of approach (CPA), more accuratly than dipole and multipolar receivers, in simulation and in lake experiment.
